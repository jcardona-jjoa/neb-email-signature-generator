When you paste, use the ordinary paste command.
That is Ctrl+V on Windows, or Command+V on a Mac. Do not use "paste as plain text" (Ctrl+Shift+V or Command+Shift+V), and do not paste into Notepad, TextEdit or Word along the way. Any of those will strip out your photo, the seal and the formatting, and leave you with plain text.

Outlook on a Windows computer

  1. Go to File, then Options, then Mail, then Signatures.
  2. Click New and give it a name, such as "Jack and Jill".
  3. Click inside the large Edit signature box and press Ctrl+V.
  4. If a small Paste Options button appears, choose Keep Source Formatting.
  5. On the right, set the signature for New messages and for Replies/forwards.
  6. Click OK.

Outlook on the web, or the new Outlook for Windows

  1. Click the settings gear at the top right, then Mail, then Compose and reply.
  2. Click inside the Email signature box and press Ctrl+V.
  3. Underneath, select your signature for new messages and for replies.
  4. Click Save. It will not keep your signature until you do.

Outlook on a Mac

  1. Open the Outlook menu, then Settings, then Signatures.
  2. Click + to add a signature and give it a name.
  3. Click inside the editing area and press Command+V.
  4. Close the window, then choose your signature under Choose default signature.

Gmail

  1. Click the settings gear, then See all settings.
  2. On the General tab, scroll down to Signature and click Create new.
  3. Give it a name, click into the box on the right, and press Ctrl+V (Command+V on a Mac).
  4. Under Signature defaults, select it for new emails and for replies.
  5. Scroll to the very bottom of the page and click Save Changes. Gmail will not keep it otherwise.

iPhone and iPad

  1. Apple Mail usually strips the formatting when you paste directly, so set it up on a computer first.
  2. Send yourself an email using your new signature.
  3. On your phone, open that email, select the whole signature and copy it.
  4. Go to Settings, then Apps, then Mail, then Signature, and paste it in.

If your photo or the seal does not appear

  1. Many email apps hide images until you allow them. Click Download pictures or Show images if you see that prompt.
  2. People you write to may see the same prompt on their end. Your name, title and contact details still show either way.
